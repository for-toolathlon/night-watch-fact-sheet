## 3) 18th-Century Story — Cross-Check (Wikipedia + Rijksmuseum)

**When painted:** Commissioned around 1639 by Captain Frans Banninck Cocq and seventeen members of his civic militia guards (*schutterij*); completed in 1642. Wikipedia says "completed in 1642 at the peak of the Dutch Golden Age" and "commissioned around 1639."

**Moving to Amsterdam's town hall:** The painting first hung in the Great Hall of Amsterdam's Kloveniersdoelen (Musketeers' Meeting Hall). In **1715** it was moved to the Amsterdam Town Hall for a trim fitting it between two columns. This was 173 years after its completion (1715 − 1642 = 173 years).

**Notable details the web sources add (Rijksmuseum/Wikipedia):**
- Trimmed on all four sides in 1715, "presumably to fit the painting between two columns"; altered since before the 19th century.
- Resulted in the loss of two characters on the left side, the top of the arch, the balustrade, and the edge of the step (the original composition has 34 total characters).
- Commissioned to hang in the banquet hall of the newly built Kloveniersdoelen (some suggest the occasion was the 1638 visit of French queen Marie de' Medici).
- Thought to have been painted in a lean-to in Rembrandt's garden, as it was too large for his Amsterdam studio.
- Eighteen names appear on a shield painted circa 1715 in the centre-right background (the hired drummer was added for free).
- A 17th-century copy by Gerrit Lundens shows the original composition.
- Post-1715 history: Trulipenhuis → Trippenhuis (the Trip family) → returned after Napoleon's occupation (1813) → again the Trippenhuis → moved to the Rijksmuseum when its building finished in 1885. The painting was removed in September 1939, stored in a safe in the caves of Maastricht for the war, and returned after the war.
- Notable: Napoleon ordered it returned after the occupation ended in 1813.

**Other notable additions:** The painting's records note 34 characters in total and that the shield names were painted later (circa 1715); the Rijksmuseum record lists the title as "The Night Watch Militia Company of District II under the Command of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq" (formerly "Officers and other civic guardsmen").
